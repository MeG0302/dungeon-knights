How a run works

The whole loop, the one screen that was missing from it, and the arithmetic that stops a sixth run on the same day. Everything here is read off the shipped code — nothing is aspirational.

The loop

Why five is the limit — the arithmetic

Two numbers decide whether a squad may enter, and they live in different places:

NumberWhere it comes fromWhat it means
runsRemaining(id) the game contract — runsRemaining() claims this knight still has in the current reset-day
bankedToday(id) this browser — the unclaimed runs it is holding clears finished today that have not been claimed yet
runs left today = runsRemaining − bankedToday, floored at 0

Claiming spends the claims and empties the queue in the same move, so the number does not move when a player claims — which is what makes it a fact about play rather than a guess about the payout. Without the subtraction, a player banks five clears, claims them, and the sixth is refused on chain after having been played.

Common and Uncommon get 5, Rare 4, Epic 3, Legendary 4 — the contract's dailyCap ladder, mirrored in the page config and in run-budget.js. Genesis Knights are counted in their own space, a flat 4 a day.

Five places that agree, in the order a player meets them

1
The roster card. Prints the chain's answer minus what this browser has banked; it says "—" while the chain has not replied, instead of a full bar it cannot vouch for.public/menu.js · public/characters.js
2
The Play button. Re-reads the chain for the selected knights before leaving the hall, and refuses a squad with a spent knight — one tired knight means nobody in the run gets paid.public/menu.js
3
Deploy, and the auto-progress. Both ask for the squad's budget first. When the day is spent the squad rests, the countdown stops, and the modal says why.public/game.js
4
The server's signature. When V4 is configured, a run cannot be priced or signed unless every knight in it has a run left: inspectSquad() reads ownership and runsRemaining on chain and refuses the rest.lib/game-runs.js · app/api/game/complete/route.js
5
The contract. The last word, and the only one that cannot be edited from a browser: require(ks.runsUsed < dailyCap[rarity], "No runs left today").contracts/DungeonKnightsGameV4.sol · …V3-Simple.sol

The first three are the courtesy layer — they stop a player wasting a dungeon. The last two are the enforcement, and neither can be talked out of it by a page. Rungs 4 and 5 are switched on by different things: V4's signature path is fully written but inert until GAME_CONTRACT_V4 is set, and until then the contract's own require is what protects the treasury.
